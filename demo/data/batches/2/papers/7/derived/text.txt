Learning Collision-Free Routes for Warehouse Robot Fleets

Warehouse robots share narrow aisles, and routes planned one robot at a time cause deadlocks at busy intersections. We train a shared routing policy with deep reinforcement learning in which each robot sees a local occupancy map of nearby robots. In a simulated warehouse with 120 robots, orders are picked 18% faster than with a priority-based planner and no deadlocks occur.


multi-robot systems, reinforcement learning, logistics



Introduction
Fulfilment centers run hundreds of mobile robots that carry shelves to picking stations. Planning each robot’s route in turn is fast but leads to congestion and deadlocks where aisles cross. Reinforcement learning  can learn behavior that anticipates other robots, and deep Q-networks  have shown that such policies scale to large observation spaces. Earlier work applied annealing-based optimizers to warehouse planning .


Method
Every robot runs the same policy. Its observation is an 11 by 11 occupancy grid centered on itself, showing shelves, walls and other robots, plus the direction to its goal. Actions are the four moves and waiting. The reward is minus one per step, minus ten for entering a cell that another robot also enters, and plus fifty on reaching the goal. We trained for 20 million steps with Adam , adding robots gradually from 10 to 120.
