Graph Convolution for Short-Term Road Speed Prediction in Dense City Grids

Navigation services predict road speed a few minutes ahead from loop detectors and probe vehicles. We model the road network as a graph and combine graph convolution with a recurrent layer, so a jam spreading from one junction informs the forecast on its neighbors. On 1,800 road segments the model reduces the 15-minute error by 14% over a per-segment recurrent baseline.


traffic prediction, graph neural networks, intelligent transport



Introduction
Congestion travels along roads: a blocked junction slows the segments that feed it a few minutes later. Forecasting each segment on its own ignores this, while treating the city as an image ignores that two adjacent pixels may be streets with no connection.
Graph convolution  passes information along edges of a graph, which matches how traffic moves. We combine it with a recurrent layer  that tracks how each segment changes over time.


Method
Nodes are road segments and edges join segments that share a junction, directed in the driving direction. Each node carries the last hour of five-minute average speeds. Two graph convolution layers mix each node with its neighbors up to two junctions away, and a gated recurrent layer turns the sequence of mixed features into a forecast for the next 15, 30 and 60 minutes. We trained with Adam  on six months of data.

Conclusion
Treating the road network as a graph lets a forecaster see congestion coming from upstream. The gain over per-segment models is largest exactly when drivers need the forecast most, at the start of a jam. Deep learning methods  keep improving here, and the graph structure is a cheap, strong prior to build on.
